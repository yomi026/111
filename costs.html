ONCE OR TWICE A YEAR

Medical care you should budget for.

Yearly and one-off medical costs
Item Typical cost How often Notes
Vaccinations $60–120 Yearly booster Rabbits need RHDV and myxomatosis cover
Parasite prevention $60–150 a year Monthly or quarterly Fleas, ticks and worms; rabbits and hamsters differ
Neutering $80–250 Once Usually before six months; ask the shelter
Dental work $100–400 As needed Rabbits and hamsters need teeth checked every visit
Emergency visit $300–1500 Unpredictable This is why the buffer exists

Keep a separate emergency fund

  • Aim for at least $500 per cat or rabbit, $1000 per dog
  • Top it up monthly, even $20 at a time
  • Insurance helps with big bills but rarely covers everything
  • Ask your clinic about payment plans before you need one

An animal that cannot get treatment because the money is not there is the most common reason pets are surrendered.

SPEND WELL

Where to save, where not to.

✅

Fine to save on

Second-hand crates, carriers and bowls. Homemade toys from cardboard boxes. Grooming at home instead of a salon, if you learn the basics.

🛒

Buy in bulk

Litter, hay and dry food are much cheaper in large bags. Store them sealed and dry, and check the expiry date before a big order.

🚫

Never cut these

Vet care, vaccinations, parasite control and species-appropriate food. Cheap food and skipped check-ups turn into expensive problems.

📉

Prevention is cheaper

A yearly check-up costs far less than treating a disease found late. Dental disease, obesity and parasites are all cheaper to prevent.
