Four ways to show you care.

A SIMPLE ROUTINE

What a good week looks like.

Most problems begin as small habits that got skipped.

Every day

  • Fresh water in a clean bowl
  • Measured food at set times
  • Scoop the litter or spot-clean bedding
  • 20–60 minutes of play or a walk
  • A quick look at eyes, nose, coat and droppings

Every week

  • Brush the coat and check skin and nails
  • Deep clean the tray, cage or bedding
  • Wash bowls and water bottles
  • Rotate toys so they stay interesting
  • Weigh small pets — sudden loss is an early warning

Every month

  • Trim nails if they are overgrown
  • Check parasite prevention is up to date
  • Restock food and litter before running out
  • Note weight and any behaviour changes
  • Set a little aside for vet costs

Before day one

  • The food your pet already eats
  • A carrier or travel box
  • Bed, litter tray or deep bedding
  • Bowls, nail clippers and a brush
  • ID tag or microchip registration
  • A vet contact saved in your phone
THROUGH THE YEAR

Care that changes with the season.

Small adjustments, big difference.

☀️

Hot weather

Water in two places, shade all day, walk dogs at dawn or dusk. Never leave an animal in a parked car. Rabbits and hamsters overheat quickly — move cages off sunny windowsills.

❄️

Cold weather

Short-coated and elderly dogs feel the cold: dry them after walks and check paws for grit. Keep small pets away from draughts, and give rabbits a dry, sheltered hutch area with extra bedding.

🌧️

Rainy weeks

Indoor play matters: food puzzles, cardboard boxes, short training sessions. A bored dog chews furniture; five minutes of sniffing games tires them more than a wet walk.

🧳

Holidays and travel

Book the sitter before you book the trip. Leave food, medication and vet details in writing, plus a signed permission note for treatment. Never leave a pet alone for days.

A LITTLE MORE KNOWLEDGE

Care for every kind of friend.

For cat parents

Cat Care

Provide fresh water, balanced cat food, a clean litter box, scratching surfaces, playtime, and regular veterinary checkups.

For dog parents

Dog Care

Plan suitable walks, positive training, social interaction, grooming, fresh water, and preventive veterinary care.

For rabbit parents

Rabbit Care

Offer unlimited grass hay, suitable leafy greens, clean water, room to move, safe hiding spaces, and rabbit-savvy veterinary care.

For hamster parents

Hamster Care

Provide an appropriately sized secure enclosure, deep bedding, a suitable wheel, enrichment, and a quiet place to sleep during the day.

Foods that can hurt.

Keep these out of reach, and tell guests too.

Phone the vet today if you notice

  • A rabbit that has not eaten or passed droppings for 12 hours
  • A male cat straining in the litter tray with little or no urine
  • Laboured breathing, collapse, a seizure or a swollen belly
  • Repeated vomiting, bloody stool or blood in the urine
  • Any pet that may have eaten something from the table above

When in doubt, call. Describing symptoms early is far cheaper than waiting a day.
