BRINGING A FRIEND HOME

Adopt with your eyes open.

A good match is decided before the first cuddle — by asking honest questions about time, money and space.

Home / Adoption
START WITH THE RIGHT QUESTION

Adopt first, buy last.

Shelters are full of animals that were bought on impulse.

🏠

Why shelters first

Rescue animals are usually health-checked, vaccinated and sometimes already house-trained. Adoption fees are far below the cost of buying, and you free a space for another animal.

❓

Ask the awkward questions

Why was the animal given up? How does it behave around children, other pets and strangers? Any known medical issues? A good shelter answers all of it without flinching.

🕰️

Expect a settling period

Rescue animals often need two to four weeks before their real personality shows. Plan for a quiet first month, not a perfect first day.

THE PROCESS

Five steps to a good match.

  1. Decide what your week allows

    Two hours a day for a dog, about one for a cat or rabbit, half an hour for a hamster. If your schedule changes month to month, choose the lower-maintenance end.

  2. Check the practical side first

    Lease, deposit, landlord permission, room size, who feeds the pet when you travel. Sorting this before meeting an animal avoids heartbreak later.

  3. Meet more than once

    Sit with the animal, walk it if it is a dog, and ask to see how it behaves outside the kennel. One visit in a busy room tells you very little.

  4. Read the paperwork

    Vaccination record, microchip number, neutering status, known conditions, and what the shelter's return policy is if the match fails.

  5. Prepare the house, then collect

    Food the animal already eats, a carrier, a bed, bowls, litter or bedding, and a vet appointment within the first week. Have it all set up before arrival day.

THE FIRST DAYS

What the first 72 hours look like.

Slow is fast. Rushing introductions causes most early problems.

Day one

  • One quiet room, door closed
  • Water, food, litter tray, bed
  • No visitors, no carrying around
  • Let the animal hide if it wants to

Days two to three

  • Sit quietly in the room, read out loud
  • Offer food by hand, no grabbing
  • Let it explore one extra room
  • Watch eating, drinking and toilet habits

First week

  • Vet visit and register the microchip
  • Start a simple routine of meals and play
  • Introduce one calm friend or family member
  • Keep the carrier out, open, as a safe spot

First month

  • Gradual introductions to other pets
  • Short, positive training sessions
  • Note weight, appetite and behaviour changes
  • Book the next vaccination or check-up
SHARING THE HOUSE

Introducing a resident pet.

🚪

Separate first

Keep the newcomer in one room for the first few days. Let them smell each other under the door before they ever see each other.

🧣

Swap scents

Rub a cloth on one animal and let the other smell it. Repeat daily. Familiar smell makes the first meeting far calmer.

🍽️

Feed on both sides of a door

Meals on either side of a closed door build a positive association. Move the bowls closer only when both animals eat calmly.

⏸️

Short meetings, easy exits

Five minutes at a time, with an escape route for each animal. End on a calm note, never after a hiss or a scuffle.

BEFORE YOU SIGN

Questions worth asking the shelter.

Ask these out loud

  • What food is the animal eating now, and how much per day?
  • Is it vaccinated, microchipped and neutered — and do I get the records?
  • Any known illness, allergy or medication?
  • How does it react to children, dogs, cats, loud noise and being alone?
  • Why was it surrendered, and has it been returned before?
  • What happens if the adoption does not work out?

If the answers are vague, wait. There is always another animal that fits better.
