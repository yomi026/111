BEFORE YOU ADOPT

Is a pet right for you right now?

Four honest questions worth answering before the first cuddle.

⏰

Time

Cats and rabbits need about an hour a day. Dogs need one to two hours for walks, training and company. Hamsters need less, but still a daily check and fresh water.

🏠

Space

Renters should check the lease and any pet deposit first. Cats want vertical space to climb, rabbits need floor room to hop, and hamsters need a large enclosure with deep bedding — not a small cage.

💳

Budget

Plan for food, litter or bedding, grooming and routine vet care every month, plus a savings buffer for one unexpected visit. Costs vary a lot by country and species.

📅

Years, not weeks

Hamsters live 2–3 years, rabbits 8–12, dogs 10–15 and cats 12–18. Decide in advance who cares for the pet during holidays, moves or a change of job.

COMMON QUESTIONS

What people ask first.

How much time does a pet need every day?

Roughly 20–30 minutes for a hamster, about an hour for a cat or rabbit, and one to two hours for a dog. Add a little extra each week for cleaning, grooming and supply runs.

What does it cost each month?

Food and bedding are the steady costs; routine vet visits, vaccinations and parasite control come once or twice a year. Keep a separate buffer for emergencies — an unexpected visit can cost far more than a month of food.

I rent my home. Can I keep a pet?

Check your lease and ask the landlord in writing. Many places allow cats or small pets but charge a deposit, and some ban dogs above a certain size. Getting permission first avoids a painful rehoming later.

What about holidays and travel?

Arrange a sitter or boarding before you book. Cats can be visited once or twice a day; rabbits need daily hay and water checks; dogs usually need company or a stay-over. No pet should be left alone for days.

Which pet suits a first-time owner with a busy job?

A calm adult cat or a pair of rabbits is often a gentler starting point than a puppy. Puppies need frequent toilet breaks, training and socialisation in their first months — hard to juggle with long office hours.
